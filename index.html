Welcome

I am an environmental geochemist working at the boundary between aqueous chemistry and atmospheric science. My research asks how acidic an aerosol particle really is, how much water it holds, and what that means for the reactions that happen inside it, with a particular interest in the oxidation of iron(II).

Since July 2026 I have been a postdoctoral researcher at the Environmental Science Research Center of Chiang Mai University, where I study the chemistry of transboundary biomass-burning haze in Northern Thailand. I estimate size-resolved aerosol pH and aerosol liquid water content (ALWC) with thermodynamic models such as E-AIM, ISORROPIA and AIOMFAC, with the aim of improving how these models represent haze-period aerosol.

I completed my PhD in Environmental Geochemistry at Imperial College London in 2026, where I quantified how pH, temperature and speciation jointly control Fe(II) oxidation kinetics in oxygenated solutions. Along the way I developed a temperature-dependent nonlinear calibration for glass pH electrodes that extends reliable measurement to negative pH. I remain a visiting researcher at Imperial, collaborating on negative-pH chemistry and advising PhD students on computational atmospheric modelling.

I am always happy to hear from potential collaborators, students, or anyone curious about aerosol acidity. Please get in touch.

Research

My work combines laboratory experiments, thermodynamic and kinetic modelling, and field aerosol measurements. Four threads run through it.

Aerosol acidity and liquid water during biomass-burning haze

Every dry season, agricultural and forest fires blanket Northern Thailand in haze. I measure size-resolved aerosol composition in Chiang Mai during haze and non-haze periods and use thermodynamic models to estimate particle pH and aerosol liquid water content. Because pH controls which reactions can proceed inside a particle, these estimates matter for secondary aerosol formation, metal solubility and health-relevant chemistry. A central goal is to test and improve how current thermodynamic models handle haze-period aerosol.

E-AIM · ISORROPIA · AIOMFAC · size-resolved sampling

Fe(II) oxidation kinetics across pH and temperature

The oxidation of ferrous iron by dissolved oxygen is one of the most pH-sensitive reactions in environmental chemistry, yet rate laws for very acidic, sulphate-rich conditions have been poorly constrained. My PhD produced a coupled pH–temperature logistic rate law for Fe(II) oxidation in sulphuric acid media spanning pH −1.5 to 8.0 and 5 to 65 °C, and an experimental–computational framework for how ligands and acid systems alter the kinetics. The results feed into models of iron bioavailability in atmospheric aerosol and of sulphate-rich waters such as acid mine drainage.

UV–Vis colorimetry · PHREEQC · MINTEQ · HySS · DFT (Gaussian)

Measuring pH where the scale breaks down

Glass pH electrodes become nonlinear in concentrated acids and alkalis, which has limited quantitative work at negative pH. I developed a temperature-dependent nonlinear calibration, improved the statistical treatment of acid and alkaline errors over pH −4.5 to 15.5, and derived transferable correction factors so that other laboratories can apply the method without re-deriving it. This work includes open calibration software presented at Goldschmidt 2025.

ion-selective electrodes · Metrohm Titrando · Python · MATLAB

Adsorption kinetics of arsenic on metal oxides

As an undergraduate researcher (UROP) at Imperial College London I co-authored a study that revised the widely used pseudo-second-order (PSO) kinetic model for adsorption so that it explicitly accounts for changes in both adsorbate and adsorbent concentrations, rather than assuming a fixed excess of one over the other. To test the model, I extracted and meta-analysed 36 rate constants from 14 independent studies of arsenic adsorption onto Fe2O3 and TiO2, giving a quantitative picture of kinetic variability across systems. The work was published in Langmuir (ACS, 2021) and the dataset is openly available on Zenodo.

kinetic modelling · meta-analysis · Langmuir 2021 · Zenodo dataset

Earlier work: a global biogeochemical analysis of arsenic speciation in the oceans drawing on more than 10,000 observations (MSci thesis, Imperial College London; departmental Centenary Prize).
