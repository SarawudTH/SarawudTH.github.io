Arsenic Pollution

Research theme · arsenic in rivers, oceans and treatment systems

How arsenic moves through water and onto mineral surfaces, from the kinetics of its removal by iron and titanium oxides to its speciation and anthropogenic signature in the global ocean.

Overview

Arsenic is one of the most widespread toxic contaminants in natural water, released by mining, agriculture and the weathering of arsenic-bearing minerals. Its fate is controlled by adsorption onto metal oxides and by its chemical speciation, both of which are the basis of water-treatment technologies and of how arsenic is transported from rivers into the sea.

This theme spans my earliest research: a kinetic model for arsenic adsorption developed as an undergraduate researcher, and my MSci thesis, which compiled more than 10,000 ocean observations to assess how human activity has altered arsenic speciation and cycling in the oceans.
