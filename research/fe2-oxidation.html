Fe(II) Oxidation

Research theme · iron redox chemistry in polluted environments

How fast ferrous iron is oxidised by oxygen in acidic, polluted water and aerosol, and what that means for iron solubility, bioavailability and the supply of iron to the oceans.

Overview

Iron is an essential micronutrient, and much of the iron that reaches the open ocean arrives through the atmosphere, carried in dust and combustion aerosol. Whether that iron is useful to marine life depends on its chemical form, and the balance between soluble Fe(II) and far less soluble Fe(III) is set by oxidation reactions that are extremely sensitive to pH, temperature and the ligands present.

This theme covers my work on measuring and modelling those oxidation kinetics, from strongly acidic sulphate-rich solutions that mimic polluted aerosol through to near-neutral water, and on turning the results into rate laws that atmospheric and geochemical models can use.
